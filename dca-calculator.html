What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, regardless of the current price, instead of trying to time the market with a single lump-sum purchase. Over time, this smooths out the average price you pay, since you buy more coins when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DCA better than investing a lump sum?

Neither is universally better — it depends on market conditions and personal risk tolerance. DCA reduces the risk of investing everything right before a downturn, but a lump sum invested at the very start of an uptrend would outperform. DCA is generally viewed as a simpler, lower-stress approach for volatile assets like crypto.

Does this calculator use real historical prices?

Yes — it looks up the actual market price on each simulated purchase date and buys accordingly, rather than assuming every purchase happened at today's price. It does not, however, factor in exchange fees, slippage, or taxes, so treat the result as an estimate rather than an exact figure.
